How these chances work

Win chances blend two sources. The betting market: DraftKings' moneyline (or spread) from the ESPN feed, with the bookmaker's margin removed. Hockey and baseball use the moneyline only, since their ±1.5 puck and run lines say little about who wins. That counts 65% (75% for college, where team stats are rougher). Linecheck's own model: each team's season results plus home field. That counts the rest. Suggested parlays skip any pick above 97%, since no pregame pick is that certain. Each card shows both numbers under the bar, and flags when the model disagrees a lot with the market. Projected totals blend the market total the same way. Games without odds in the feed use the model alone. Projections include ESPN's injury reports (NFL starting QBs, NBA and WNBA players, NHL starting goalies, MLB hitters), MLB probable starting pitchers, and the forecast weather for football. Tap Dig in for anything else. NBA picks include back-to-back rest. While the app is open, scores and chances refresh every minute.

About and responsible play

Linecheck is an independent research tool. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to DraftKings, any sportsbook, league or team. It never takes bets or holds money; you place every bet yourself in your sportsbook app. Scores and stats come from ESPN's public feeds and odds from the sportsbook lines in those feeds. Win chances are estimates and can be wrong.

Responsible play

Set a budget before you bet and stick to it. Never chase losses or bet money you need for bills. Take breaks, and don't bet when you're upset or after drinking. If betting stops being fun, get help: call or text 1-800-GAMBLER (U.S., free, 24/7), visit ncpgambling.org, or use your sportsbook's limits and self-exclusion tools.

Weather and quarterback news

For outdoor NFL and college games, Linecheck checks the forecast at the stadium for kickoff (free data from Open-Meteo). Wind over 10 mph lowers the projected total by about a third of a point per mph, up to 8 points. Steady rain takes off about 1.5 points, snow 2.5, and temperatures below 25°F 1 more. Domes are skipped. For NFL games, if a team's starting quarterback (its season passing leader) is listed Out on ESPN's injury report, that team's projection drops 4.5 points; Doubtful counts three-quarters as much. College injury reports aren't published reliably, so college QB news isn't included. A yellow note on the game shows any change.

How injuries change the picks

NFL: a starting quarterback listed Out lowers his team 4.5 points (Doubtful, three-quarters of that). Players listed Out are left off NFL props. NBA: each player listed Out lowers his team by up to 7 points depending on how much he scores and plays (Doubtful three-quarters, Questionable about a third). Teammates' prop projections go up a little to pick up the missing scoring. WNBA: same as NBA, scaled for the WNBA. NHL: if a team's main goalie (most starts) is listed Out, the backup is expected to start and the team gives up about 0.3 more goals. MLB: each team's probable starting pitcher shifts the runs it's expected to allow, based on his ERA (blended toward league average when he hasn't pitched much, up to 1.2 runs either way). A regular hitter listed Out lowers his team's runs by up to 0.3, depending on how much he produces. A player who has missed most of the season counts less, because the team's results already reflect playing without him. Notes on each game show what changed. College football, college basketball and soccer injuries aren't included, because ESPN doesn't publish usable injury reports for them.

How college football picks work

College schedules are very uneven, so raw points scored and allowed can mislead. Linecheck rates every FBS team's offense and defense against the teams it actually played, using every FBS score this season. Early in the season, last season's ratings fill in. A team's projected points are its offense rating plus the opponent's defense rating, plus about a field goal for the home side. Win chances, spreads, totals, team totals, halves and quarters all come from these ratings. Games against FCS teams count, with FCS teams rated as one group. Blowouts are capped, so a 63–7 rout counts a bit less than its full margin and doesn't inflate a rating.

DraftKings lines in the boxes

Open Totals and team totals on a game and the game total, spread and team total boxes fill with DraftKings' current line by themselves. Live games refresh about every 10 seconds and upcoming games every 2 minutes; the status line under the DraftKings info shows the last update. During a game the app uses ESPN's DraftKings live-odds line when ESPN lists one; the bold label shows which line you're seeing. ESPN's free feed can trail DraftKings' own app by a little, so compare with DraftKings before you bet. Refreshes only redraw games whose numbers changed, so the screen stays still.

Team players

At the top of the Props tab, pick any team with a game coming up, live or just finished (it follows the sport and day picked at the top). You'll see every player with season numbers from ESPN: TDs and passing, rushing and receiving yards for football; points, rebounds, assists and threes per game for basketball; average, home runs, RBIs, ERA and strikeouts for baseball; goals, assists and points for hockey; goals and assists for soccer leaders. NFL players also show their chance to score a TD in the next game, and NBA players their projected points. When the team is playing, a Today column shows live stats that refresh every 30 seconds. Football teams (NFL and college) also list Defense (tackles, sacks, tackles for loss, interceptions, passes defended) and Special teams (field goals, extra points, return yards, punts) under the main table; Rest of the roster is linemen and players who haven't recorded a stat this season. NBA player points

On the Games tab, open Player points projection on any NBA game to see each team's main players with projected points, the chance of 20 or more, rebounds and assists. Projections blend this season with last season (early on), adjust for the opponent's defense and back-to-backs, and leave off players listed Out, sharing their points among teammates. During the game, the Live tile shows the real box score with a projected final for each player. For any other line, use the Props tab.

Who is home and who is away

Games are always listed away team first, home team second, the same as "Cowboys @ Packers" on DraftKings. Each team also has an Away or Home tag, and live tiles put an @ in front of the home team. A tag with a * means the game is at a neutral site, so neither team really has home advantage and the app doesn't give one.

Live tab and player status

The Live tab follows the sport picked at the top. It has two sub-tabs. Scores shows every game as a small tile: live games with their win chances, games starting in the next 3 hours and today's finals. Tap a tile for details, including touchdown and goal scorers and each scoring play. Player props on your tickets get a live progress bar under the player's name (for example 187/250 passing yards, with how many more are needed and the pace); it turns green when the prop hits and red when an under goes over. This works for NFL yards, catches and TDs and for NBA points, rebounds, assists and threes. Basketball tiles show every player's points, rebounds and assists, and live NBA tiles add a projected final points column (points so far plus the pregame projection for the time left; players ruled out for the game stop there). Player status shows the injury report. Upset watch flags a pregame favorite that is losing. Close finish flags a late game that is still near 50/50. Player status lists each team's injury report: Out and Suspended players won't play, Doubtful players probably won't, Questionable is close to a coin flip, and Day-to-day is minor. Left early means a news note says the player exited a game in the last day. Check status before betting a player prop. Books usually void a prop if the player doesn't play at all, but not if he leaves early.

Moneyline

Pick who wins. Negative odds show what you risk to win $100; positive odds show what $100 wins.

-150: bet $150 to win $100 · +130: bet $100 to win $130
Point spread

The favorite must win by more than the spread; the underdog covers by losing by less (or winning). Most spreads are priced near -110 on both sides, so you need to win about 52.4% to break even.

Steelers -3.5: they must win by 4 or more
Total (over/under)

Bet on the combined score of both teams going over or under a number. Weather, pace and injuries to offensive stars move totals most.

O/U 44.5: a 24-21 final (45) goes over
Parlay

Several bets tied into one. Every leg must win. Payouts grow fast, but so does the book's edge, because the margin compounds on each leg.

Three -110 legs pay about +596, but hit only ~13% if each is a true coin flip
Teaser

A parlay where you move the spreads in your favor (usually 6 points in football) in exchange for a smaller payout. The classic version crosses the key numbers 3 and 7.

Two-team 6-pt teaser: +7.5 becomes +1.5 underdog → +13.5
Game props (team yards, first score, FGs, margin)

Every NFL and college game that hasn't started has a Game props section, matching DraftKings' game props: each team's passing, rushing and total yards (season yards per game against what the other defense allows), first team to score and first scoring play (TD, field goal or safety), total and team field goals, winning margin (1–6, 7–12, 13–18, 19+ for each team), overtime and safety. Team TDs are in TD props. Type DraftKings' line in any box and only bet when DraftKings pays more than the fair odds. These are for checking value; to track one, add it in Bets › My bets by hand.

TD props (longest TD, total TDs)

Longest touchdown: which team scores the longest TD of the game, by yards. "Neither" wins only if nobody scores a TD. Total touchdowns: all TDs by both teams, including return and defensive TDs; Over 7.5 means 8 or more. Every NFL and college game that hasn't started has a TD props section with the chance and fair odds for each, built from the projected score. Type DraftKings' line in a total TDs box (or use − / +) and only bet when DraftKings pays more than the fair odds.

Team total

An over/under on one team's points only, not both teams. Open “Totals and team totals” on any game to compare DraftKings' line with the app's projection.

Chiefs team total 26.5: Chiefs score 27+ = over
Props

Bets on events inside a game, like a player's passing yards or who scores first. Books often have bigger margins on props.

Anytime, first and next TD

Anytime TD pays if the player scores a touchdown at any point. First TD pays only if he scores the game's first touchdown. Next TD is a live bet on who scores the next touchdown from now. First and next TD pay much more because so many players could score.

The Props tab shows Anytime and First TD before kickoff, and a live tracker with Next TD during games
Push

When the result lands exactly on the line, the bet is refunded. Half-point lines (like 3.5) can't push.

Key numbers

NFL games end with a 3- or 7-point margin far more often than other margins, so a half point around 3 or 7 is worth more than one around 5.

Halves and quarters

Every NFL, college and NBA game has a “Halves and quarters” section with the projected score, winner chance, model spread and total for the 1st half, 2nd half and each quarter. During a game, finished periods show the final score and the current period updates live. Pick a period, then tap − / + to set DraftKings' total or spread and see your chance. Use ± to switch a spread between plus and minus. Football quarters end tied fairly often, which usually pushes a 2-way quarter bet.

Soccer bets, plainly

Moneyline (3-way)

Pick the home team, the away team or a draw, after 90 minutes plus stoppage time. If you back a team and it ends in a draw, you lose.

Arsenal +120 · Draw +250 · Chelsea +230
Double chance and draw no bet

Double chance covers two of the three results, like “Arsenal or draw”. Draw no bet refunds your stake if it's a draw. Both pay less but win more often.

Total goals

Over or under a number of goals, most often 2.5. Over 2.5 needs at least 3 goals.

Both teams to score

Yes wins if each team scores at least once. The final score doesn't matter.

Correct score and 1st half

Correct score pays big but rarely hits. 1st half bets cover only the first 45 minutes, which usually has fewer goals than the second half.

Goalscorer bets

Anytime goalscorer pays if the player scores at any point in 90 minutes. First goalscorer pays only if he scores the match's first goal. A player who doesn't start usually voids or loses the bet depending on the book's rules, so check the lineups before betting.

Spread (Asian handicap)

Soccer's version of a spread, like -0.5 or +1.5 goals. A -0.5 is the same as picking that team to win outright.

Golf and racing bets

Outright winner

Pick who wins the whole tournament or race. Big fields mean long odds: even the favorite often wins less than 1 in 4 times. Use Odds check on the event card to type DraftKings' odds and see whether the price is worth it.

Top 5, top 10, top 20 and podium

Your player only has to finish in that group. In golf, ties at the cut-off are “dead heats” and pay a fraction, which lowers what these bets are really worth.

Matchups

Pick which of two players or drivers finishes ahead. The rest of the field doesn't matter. Use Matchup on the event card.

1st-round leader (golf)

Who leads after Thursday's round. Lots of players are tied or close after one round, so prices are long.

Tennis and UFC bets

Tennis: match winner and set betting

Match winner is a moneyline on the match. Set betting picks the exact score in sets, like 2-0 or 2-1. Total sets over 2.5 wins if a best-of-3 match goes to a third set. Grand Slam men's matches are best of 5.

Tennis: how the app rates players

It uses official ranking points, which reward results over the past year. It doesn't know about surface, recent form or injuries, so a player returning from injury can look weaker than they are.

UFC: winner, distance and rounds

“Goes the distance” means the fight reaches the judges' scorecards. Total rounds over 1.5 means it lasts past the halfway point of round 2. Method bets combine who wins with how, by finish (KO or submission) or decision.

UFC: how the app rates fighters

It uses each fighter's win-loss record and how often fights in that weight class end early. Records don't show who a fighter beat, so these numbers are rougher than the other sports.

Baseball and hockey bets

Run line and puck line

Baseball and hockey's version of a spread, almost always 1.5. The favorite at -1.5 must win by 2 or more. Game cards show the model's chance for the favorite to cover -1.5.

Totals

Runs or goals for both teams, often 8.5 in baseball and 5.5 or 6.5 in hockey. Open “Totals and team totals” on any game to check DraftKings' number.

Starting pitchers and goalies

In baseball the starting pitcher changes the odds a lot, and in hockey the starting goalie does. The app doesn't know who's starting yet, so check that before you bet.

NBA bets, plainly

Moneyline

Pick who wins, overtime included. Big NBA favorites can cost -400 or more, so a moneyline parlay of favorites is popular but pays little per leg.

Celtics -250: bet $250 to win $100
Point spread

The most bet NBA market. The favorite must win by more than the spread. NBA margins spread out more evenly than football, so there are no big key numbers, but half points still matter in close games.

Knicks -6.5: they must win by 7 or more
Total (over/under)

Combined points for both teams, usually between 210 and 240. Fast-paced teams, back-to-backs, and missing stars move totals most. Overtime counts.

O/U 226.5: a 115-112 final (227) goes over
Player points, rebounds, assists

The most popular NBA props. Bet a player over or under a line, like 24.5 points. DraftKings also offers milestones, like “25+ points”, which is the same idea paid at fixed odds. The Props tab shows the chance of each.

Over 24.5 points: he needs 25 or more
3-pointers made

How many threes a player makes. Low numbers swing a lot from night to night, so even good shooters miss a 3+ line often.

PRA (points + rebounds + assists)

One line for a player's points, rebounds and assists added together. It smooths out a bad night in one stat, which is why many bettors like it.

25 pts + 9 reb + 6 ast = 40 PRA
Double-double and triple-double

Double-double means 10 or more in two of points, rebounds and assists. Triple-double means all three. Big men who rebound well hit double-doubles most often.

Quarters, halves and first basket

Spreads and totals for just one quarter or half, and bets on who scores first. These are quick but have more luck and bigger book margins.

Same-game parlay

Several bets from one game in one ticket, like a player's points plus the over. They're popular, but the book prices in how the legs are linked, so the margin is usually bigger than a normal parlay.

Rest and injuries

NBA stars sit more often than in football, and teams often confirm who plays only 30 minutes before tip-off. Teams on the second night of a back-to-back tend to play worse. Always check the injury report before betting player props.

How the model works

Each team's points for minus points against gives its strength per game. Early in the season that's pulled toward average, since a few games say little. Home field adds 1.5 points. A backup QB costs 5.5 points, each other key player out costs 1 point, a bye adds 1 and a short week costs 1. The resulting margin turns into a win chance using the typical NFL game-to-game spread of about 13.5 points.

MLB, NHL, WNBA and college basketball: the same idea as the NBA. Runs, goals or points scored minus allowed per game, blended with last season until enough games are played, plus home field and rest. College basketball doesn't adjust for strength of schedule yet, so small-conference teams can look better than they are.

Soccer: each team's goals scored and allowed per game, compared with its league average and pulled toward average early in the season. Home teams get about a 12% scoring boost. Those expected goals feed a Poisson model that gives every score's chance, which adds up to win, draw, totals and both-teams-to-score chances. During a game it uses the current score and the minutes left.

NBA: each team's points scored minus points allowed per game, blended with last season (pulled 40% toward average for roster changes) until about 10 games are played. Home court adds 2.5 points, and a team on the second night of a back-to-back loses 1.5. The margin becomes a win chance using a game-to-game spread of about 12.5 points. Projected totals come from each team's scoring and defense. Player projections use season averages, adjusted for the opponent's defense and rest.
